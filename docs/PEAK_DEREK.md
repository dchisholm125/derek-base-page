# Peak Derek

## Mission

Build the conditions in which Derek can consistently become the strongest, most capable, confident, present, adventurous, and effective version of himself while protecting the people and responsibilities that matter most.

Peak Derek is not a financial-maximization framework. Money, career, technology, health, and lifestyle are tools. The objective is a life with high capability, strong relationships, meaningful work, resilience, and room to enjoy what has been built.

## Operating functions

### Mental performance
Maintain clarity, confidence, resilience, and sound judgment. Treat support, recovery, and reflection as performance infrastructure rather than afterthoughts.

### Career
Pursue work that improves competence, compensation, responsibility, and future optionality. Favor roles where difficult problems, learning, and ownership compound over time.

### Family
Protect presence, stability, connection, and shared experiences. A plan that improves a spreadsheet while making family life materially worse is not an optimal plan.

### Health & energy
Protect sleep, movement, recovery, and sustainable energy. High output that cannot be sustained is not peak performance.

### Finance
Use money to create resilience and choices. Protect liquidity during uncertainty, remove expensive debt when income is stable, build reserves, invest consistently, and fund lifestyle goals only from a strong base.

### Life & adventure
Keep room for skiing, golf, hiking, travel, friends, outdoors, curiosity, and fun. Enjoyment is a permanent operating function, not a reward deferred until some distant finish line.

### Long-term dreams
Pursue ambitious projects, homes, travel, and other lifestyle goals when they expand life without undermining the foundation beneath them.

## Sequencing rules

1. **Protect the floor during uncertainty.** Preserve liquidity, health, relationships, and required obligations before making aggressive commitments.
2. **Increase capability and reliable income.** Career growth is often the highest-leverage financial and personal move.
3. **Remove fragile debt.** Once income is reliable and a cash floor exists, eliminate expensive consumer debt aggressively.
4. **Build resilience.** Establish meaningful emergency reserves and predictable sinking funds for known future costs.
5. **Automate the good life.** Fund retirement, investing, family experiences, travel, and everyday quality of life intentionally.
6. **Then fund major optional dreams.** Second homes, large lifestyle assets, and other long-duration commitments should come from strength rather than urgency.

## Decision rules

- Optimize the whole life, not a single metric.
- Prefer optionality over fragile leverage.
- Separate facts from fear before making irreversible decisions.
- Do not make major life or financial decisions in a severely impaired state unless immediate safety requires action.
- Use professional expertise where the problem is clinical, legal, tax, medical, or otherwise specialized.
- Consistency beats unsustainable intensity.
- A higher income is valuable only if it improves the broader system.
- Quality of life belongs in the plan now, at a level the current foundation can support.
- Long-term dreams remain visible even when they are not yet funded.

## Operating states

### Green — Attack
Energy and clarity are strong. Push on the highest-leverage work: difficult problems, interviews, learning, training, building, and important decisions.

### Yellow — Execute
Energy or confidence is imperfect but functional. Do not redesign everything. Follow the existing plan, complete the next important actions, and protect basic routines.

### Red — Protect
Function is materially impaired. Reduce scope, protect health and obligations, avoid unnecessary irreversible decisions, and use the appropriate support network. The objective is stabilization, not self-judgment.

## Weekly review

Ask six questions:

1. **Mind:** How was my psychological state and judgment this week?
2. **Body:** Did I protect sleep, movement, recovery, and health?
3. **Career:** Did I meaningfully increase opportunity or capability?
4. **Family:** Was I present and connected?
5. **Money:** Did I make the household more resilient or more optional?
6. **Life:** Did I do something I genuinely enjoyed?

Then ask one final question:

> What is the single biggest constraint preventing Peak Derek next week?

That constraint becomes the next operating priority.

## Definition of progress

Peak Derek is not a fixed destination. Progress means increasing the ability to choose well across work, family, health, money, and life while reducing avoidable fragility.

The scoreboard is not perfection. It is a life with more capability, more agency, stronger relationships, better judgment, and more genuine enjoyment over time.
